# PR #204 - Restore mobile Story media playback affordance

- **Source head:** `245299c7e1bcc680644f254a74bd49ca68885615`
- **Scope:** Restores the mobile Story Viewer play/pause affordance for multi-media stories and aligns Story autoplay with mixed image/video completion, browser user-activation constraints, signed-read ownership, native video controls, stall recovery, fullscreen, swipe ownership, overview morphs, and two-layer media transitions. The implementation uses one persistent stage video node per surface (inline and fullscreen), prefetches the first future video read, gates autoplay entry until that candidate read resolves, and reuses the same authorized node as a video becomes incoming and then settled.
- **User-visible change:** Mobile Story Viewer users can start or pause sequence playback without entering Manage mode. Images retain timed progression; videos play to completion, recover from a rejected `play()` or a non-terminal stall, stop Story autoplay when paused through native controls, and are not silently skipped when reached later in the sequence. Fullscreen playback uses the video node authorized by the fullscreen-entry gesture. Swiping and the overview shared-element transition both stay attached to the visible settled media rather than to hidden priming media.
- **Review fixes:** Review first caught that the new “自动播放媒体” control still behaved like a fixed 5.2s photo slideshow. Story now uses the shared playback media vocabulary, waits on video `ended`, and falls back safely on failure. Later reviews added disposal-aware protection for delayed `play()` rejection, a four-second stall watchdog, native-pause ownership release, and protection against the signed-URL refresh sweep replacing the active autoplay video's `src`. A P1 then showed that videos reached after image steps could lose transient user activation; Story now keeps a persistent stage video element, primes it inside the initiating gesture, and reuses that element through the incoming transition. Exact-head CI exposed that the first version still rendered a duplicate incoming video node, so the authorized persistent node became the transition layer itself. The CFAA family review found four coupled assumptions introduced by priming: fullscreen used a distinct node mounted after the gesture, autoplay could start before the candidate URL existed, mobile swipe could select the hidden priming node, and video→video navigation preferred the old settled video over the incoming destination. A follow-up P2 found the readiness gate too broad: a failed candidate prefetch (`error`) would permanently disable autoplay even though unavailable media already has a defined timer/fallback path. The gate now blocks only while the candidate is unresolved (`waiting`/`loading`), and `navigateToMedia()` plus pending-target promotion treat `error` as a completed unavailable step so autoplay times through it instead of hanging on the previous frame. Desktop, mobile, and fullscreen play gates all call `storyAutoplayCanStart()`. Final review then found two remaining gaps: the overview shared-element morphs still selected the first `img`/`video` under the stage and therefore resolved to the hidden priming video whenever the current image had a later video, dropping the transition in both directions; they now select through `data-shared-media-id`, matching the swipe path. The earlier conversion of fullscreen-exit QA from DOM-detached to semantically hidden was also incomplete — the mixed-media Escape exit in `qa-media-controls` and the history-back exit in `qa-story-sheet` still waited for detachment, and the former is what failed the `login-media` CI lane on the previous head. Finally, the `story-autoplay-future-video-authorization` check compared play events deserialized from two separate `page.evaluate()` calls, so its `event !== primingVideoEvent` exclusion was always true and the priming event could satisfy its own reuse assertion; it now records the event count at the gesture and compares only plays recorded after it, with the wait observing the same window. Exercising that assertion for the first time then failed the `login-media` lane on a `waitForFunction` timeout, and the root cause was in two places, neither of them the replay itself. The stage `<video>` is mounted before its own step, and its `hidden` attribute was inert because the author rule `.journey-story__media > video { display: block }` outranks the UA `[hidden]` rule: on desktop the primed node therefore took a full-height slot in the `overflow: hidden` stage and clipped the current frame away, and everywhere it reported itself visible to the browser QA scripts. The hidden state now has an explicit out-of-flow, transparent, non-interactive presentation rather than `display: none`, so the element the gesture authorized is still rendered. The check in turn had used that inert attribute as its step boundary — it waited for the primed video to become visible, which resolved immediately, and then allowed 3s for the replay while an image step lasts `STORY_AUTOPLAY_STEP_MS` (5.2s), so the wait could never reach the video step. The meaningless visibility gate is gone and the replay wait now covers a full image step plus its settle; the assertion on the recorded `play()` events, including the `elementId` identity comparison against the priming event, is unchanged.
- **Follow-up:** Real-device Safari/Chrome media-policy behavior and real decode/stall timing remain outside the synthetic CI fixture, whose video is a 4-byte data URI. The overview morph selector change is DOM-level and is covered only by browser QA, not by a node-environment unit test. Per-element authorization is retained by reusing one node per stage rather than by sharing a single element across the inline and fullscreen stages; unifying them is deferred. Safe-area right inset behavior remains pre-existing scope. No further feature expansion is included in this PR.
